When Older Siblings Struggle to Adjust to a New Baby

Start by identifying the moments when your older child feels displaced, rather than demanding instant affection.

Nana's Nest editorial draft · Caregiver review pending

Feeding-time jealousy

If your child demands attention whenever baby feeds, prepare a book or quiet activity to share while seated. Promise a specific achievable time together afterwards and follow through.

Regression and bedtime

If a toilet-trained child has accidents or bedtime becomes difficult, respond without shaming. Restore familiar routines and make individual attention predictable. Avoid adding unnecessary major transitions at the same time.

When the child is rough

Calmly separate the children, supervise all contact and state the boundary clearly: feelings are allowed, hurting is not. Do not leave them alone together while safety is uncertain.

Help without pressure

Offer optional small tasks, such as selecting clothes, but do not require affection or caregiving. Seek pediatric guidance when changes are severe, persistent or safety is difficult to maintain. See AAP age-specific sibling guidance.

These are general practical suggestions, not a report of individual client outcomes or medical advice.
